# Paper candidates — to research

Papers we want to cite in the knowledge base but haven't verified + written a note for yet. Pick from here when invoking the `summarize-paper-to-strategy` skill.

**Scope:** this library is **smart-microscopy-specific**. Adaptive / event-driven / closed-loop / AI-guided / content-aware / feedback-controlled acquisition. Foundational physics (Nyquist, Abbe, sampling theory), sensor hardware (CCD/CMOS), textbook references (Pawley handbook, Diaspro), and pure methods papers (co-localization math, phototoxicity surveys) are orientation material — they live as plain-text pointers in the concept note's `## Further reading` section, not here.

Adding an entry: one line with the **claim**, not the citation. "Event-driven microscopy that switches imaging mode on detected events" — not "Mahecic 2022". The skill then searches online for the actual paper matching the claim.

If a candidate's paper can't be verified online (no DOI, no abstract accessible), remove it from this list and note why in a commit message — we don't cite what we can't verify.

When a candidate is resolved into the index, **delete it from this file** rather than striking it through — the git log + `[[Papers index]]` are the durable record. Out-of-scope verdicts go into the Deferred section below.

### Deferred additions from 2026-04-24/25 review pass

- Sommer et al. 2017, *Mol Biol Cell* 28(23):3428–3436, "A deep learning and novelty detection framework for rapid phenotyping in high-content screening" (doi:10.1091/mbc.E17-05-0333). Verified 2026-04-24: abstract and text describe CellCognition Explorer as a post-hoc novelty-detection / deep-learning tool applied to already-acquired screening datasets. No closed-loop or acquisition-driving component. The candidate claim ("driving acquisition: image whatever the network hasn't seen before") over-reads the paper. Aspirational paradigm, not a published acquisition-control paper — in-scope analogue is Ye 2025 (uncertainty-driven rescan) or Kandel 2023 (FAST), both already in the index.

- Multimodal LLM diagnostic loops (Ward 2026 §5.1, refs 83–88). Verified 2026-04-25 as out-of-scope. Ward §5.1 cites six papers at the relevant ref numbers: Wang et al. 2024 *Commun. Eng.* 3:133 ChatCAD (doi:10.1038/s44172-024-00271-8) [83]; Zhang et al. 2023 BiomedCLIP (arXiv:2303.00915) [84]; Zhao et al. 2023 *CLIP in Medical Imaging* survey (arXiv:2312.07353) [85]; Yan et al. 2023 *Multimodal ChatGPT for Medical Applications* (arXiv:2310.19061) [86]; Van/Verma/Wu 2024 *On Large Visual Language Models for Medical Imaging Analysis* CHASE 2024:172 [87]; Verma/Van/Wu 2024 *Beyond Human Vision: The Role of Large Vision Language Models in Microscope Image Analysis* (arXiv:2405.00876) [88]. Each was verified to be a *post-hoc image-classification / VQA benchmark on already-acquired data*, not a closed-loop on-instrument acquisition controller — Ward in fact cites them to make the *negative* point that "currently these generally underperform against CNNs" and "all the available LLMs performed worse than human experts and, at times, simply refused to perform a task". Verma/Van/Wu 2024 is the only one tested on actual microscopy images, but tests ChatGPT/LLaVA/Gemini/SAM in zero-shot evaluation on classification/segmentation/counting/VQA — no microscope control. ChatCAD is interactive Q&A diagnosis on chest X-rays. The "image+text tokens → next-acquisition instructions" framing in the candidate hint is the *aspirational* reading of §5.1 — but Ward's own next-acquisition examples in §5.1 fire via Liang [90] (code-as-policies) and Boiko [91] (Coscientist), both already in this library as [[Papers/Liang 2023]] and [[Papers/Boiko 2023]], with [[Papers/Mandal 2025]] as the on-microscope evaluation and [[Papers/Kesavan 2025]] as the architectural critique. Same archetype as Sommer 2017 / Janssens 2021 above: a paradigm hint that over-reads classification-only papers into the closed-loop slot. Out of scope for this library. The in-scope analogue closest to "multimodal-encoder-driven acquisition" is [[Papers/Yu 2024]] — PLIP (a vision-language foundation model) wrapped as a survey-tile scoring head that *does* drive stage moves and objective switches; that paper occupies the slot the candidate hint described.

- Lu, Christensen, Weber, Konno, Läubli, Scherer, Avezov, Liò, Lapkin, Schierle & Kaminski 2023, *Nat. Methods* 20:569–579, "ERnet: a tool for the semantic segmentation and quantitative analysis of endoplasmic reticulum topology" (doi:10.1038/s41592-023-01815-0). Verified 2026-04-25 as the *only* reference Ward 2026 cites at the autonomous-sample-environment pen-stroke (§5.2 "Expanding the Scope of Hardware Automation", ref [46]). ERnet is a post-hoc semantic-segmentation deep-learning tool that classifies ER sheet vs. tubular domains and skeletonises them into connectivity graphs; it operates on already-acquired confocal / SIM image stacks and outputs network-topology metrics. There is no acquisition-control or sample-environment component in the paper. Ward 2026 cites it as the *health-monitor analysis output* that *could in principle* gate a closed loop adjusting reagent concentration or triggering fixation — phrased explicitly as "**could**", not "does". Out of scope for this library (offline analysis only). The image-triggered-fixation slot Ward describes is *already* occupied by [[Papers/Almada 2019]] (live-to-fixed event capture: live-imaging trigger fires mid-experiment, fluidics auto-injects fixative, switch to super-resolution protocol on the same cells). The graded reagent-concentration-tuned-by-phenotype extension has no flagship paper — programmed-cycle multiplexed fluidics ([[Papers/Lutz 2018, Rames 2023]]) and event-driven microfluidic pipelines ([[Papers/Friederich 2025]], where image-based events drive autofocus and acquisition planning but not reagent dosing) are the adjacent in-scope flagships. Optogenetic closed-loop ([[Papers/Lugagne 2024]], [[Papers/Passmore 2025]], [[Papers/Hinderling 2025]]) closes a loop on light dose, not on chemistry. Same archetype as the multimodal-LLM and Sommer 2017 deferrals: a paradigm hint that over-reads an analysis-only paper into the closed-loop slot. Out of scope for this library.

- Janssens, Zhang, Kauffmann, de Weck & Durand 2021, *Bioinformatics* 37(23):4548–4555, "Fully unsupervised deep mode of action learning for phenotyping high-content cellular images" (doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btab497). Verified 2026-04-25: paper introduces an unsupervised deep-clustering method that ingests *full pre-acquired* high-content screening images (BBBC021 + an internal "1008 Tales" dataset) and groups compounds by mode-of-action; reaches 97.09% MOA accuracy by nearest-neighbour matching against a labelled subset. Abstract is explicit that the method "works from the entire images directly" and is trained / evaluated on already-collected screening plates — no real-time, online, or closed-loop integration with the microscope. Same archetype as Sommer 2017 above: phenotype-novelty / clustering analysis on a finished image set, not acquisition control. The candidate claim ("driving acquisition: image whatever the network hasn't seen before") over-reads the paper into the aspirational curiosity-driven-RL paradigm flagged separately. Out of scope for this library. The in-scope analogue of using model uncertainty to pick the next acquisition target is Ye 2025 (uncertainty-driven rescan), with Bouchard 2023 (TA-GAN reconstruction-uncertainty trigger) and Kandel 2023 (FAST tile selection) as adjacent flagships — all already indexed.

- Pylvänäinen et al. 2023, *J Cell Sci* 136(4):jcs260728, "Fast4DReg – fast registration of 4D microscopy datasets" (doi:10.1242/jcs.260728). Verified 2026-04-24: Fast4DReg is an ImageJ/Fiji plugin; abstract explicitly frames it as a tool to "correct images post acquisition" and "before further visualization and analysis". No closed-loop or acquisition-time integration — it is a post-processing registration tool. The Pylvänäinen 2023 *Curr Opin Cell Biol* review cites it as one of several drift-correction tools but does not present it as online. Candidate claim ("integrated into the acquisition pipeline so drift is corrected online rather than post hoc") over-reads the paper. Out of scope for this library (post-hoc only). The in-scope analogue of online drift correction is Royer 2016 AutoPilot (already indexed), which maintains alignment during acquisition.

- Chen et al. 2023, *Nature Communications* 14:2854, "Single-frame deep-learning super-resolution microscopy for intracellular dynamics imaging" (doi:10.1038/s41467-023-38452-2). Verified 2026-04-24: SFSRM is a pure reconstruction method — a CNN with subpixel-edge-map guidance turns a single diffraction-limited frame into a 30 nm / 10 ms super-resolution image, replacing SMLM's thousands of frames. There is no closed-loop or acquisition-driving component; the microscope's acquisition parameters are fixed before imaging begins and the SR output is not used to decide where or when to image next. Candidate claim ("live scout to guide acquisition of real diffraction-limited data only at interesting sites") over-reads the paper. Out of scope for this library (offline reconstruction only). The in-scope analogues are Weigert 2018 CARE (trading acquisition dose for an offline restoration prior) and Jin 2020 DL-SIM (fewer raw frames per super-resolved plane) — both already indexed.

- Gallusser, Stieber & Weigert 2023, MICCAI 2023 / arXiv:2305.05511, "Self-supervised dense representation learning for live-cell microscopy with time arrow prediction" (doi:10.48550/arXiv.2305.05511; published version doi:10.1007/978-3-031-43993-3_52). Verified 2026-04-24: TAP is a self-supervised pre-training task — predict the correct temporal order of time-flipped image regions — that yields dense feature representations transferable to downstream detection / segmentation / classification of cell divisions and cell states. Abstract and code (weigertlab/tarrow) describe the method as offline representation learning evaluated on pre-recorded videos; there is no acquisition-driving or closed-loop component. The candidate claim ("as an unsupervised trigger for event-driven acquisition") over-reads the paper into an aspirational paradigm not present in the published work. Out of scope for this library. The in-scope analogues for unsupervised event triggers are Bouchard 2023 TA-GAN (reconstruction uncertainty as event trigger) and Alvelid 2022 event-triggered STED (explicit event detector firing high-resolution mode) — both already indexed.

- Autonomous sample-environment control co-scheduled with imaging (aspirational paradigm surfaced by Ward 2026 §5.2). Verified 2026-04-25 as out-of-scope. Ward's only cited reference at the relevant pen-stroke is ERnet (Lu 2023) — see deferral above; Ward's framing is "**could**", not "does". The image-triggered-fixation half is already covered by [[Papers/Almada 2019]]; programmed-cycle multiplexing is covered by [[Papers/Lutz 2018, Rames 2023]]; event-driven microfluidic pipelines by [[Papers/Friederich 2025]]. No flagship paper closes a loop on reagent concentration from live phenotype scoring.

- DL denoising (CARE variants, Noise2Void, DeepSTORM): pure post-acquisition reconstruction, not acquisition-control. CARE itself remains in scope as the progenitor of content-aware acquisition; follow-ups do not.
- SIM / SMLM / STED reconstruction primers (Gustafsson 2000, Rust 2006, Hell 1994, Betzig 2006, Balzarotti 2017 MINFLUX): imaging-geometry / physics references; belong as `## Further reading` in the super-resolution concept note, not here.
- In-silico labelling / label-free prediction (Christiansen 2018, Ounkomol 2018): post-acquisition inference, no acquisition-side feedback.
- Cell Painting protocols (Bray 2016, Cimini 2023): assay design, not microscope control.
- Connectome / neural-inventory papers (Nern 2024): a sample target, not an acquisition-control paradigm.
- Graph-neural-network analysis tools (Pineda 2023, Ben-Haim 2022): pure analysis, unless coupled to an acquisition loop.
- Synthetic data generation for microscopy (GAN / diffusion / NeRF / neural cellular automata): training-data infrastructure, not acquisition-control.
- Explainable AI / retrieval-augmented generation / sparse-autoencoder interpretability: ML foundations cited in Ward 2026 §5.4; out of scope for this library.
