## Abstract

Light-sheet microscopes enable rapid high-resolution imaging of biological specimens; however, biological processes span spatiotemporal scales. Moreover, long-term phenotypes are often instigated by rare or fleeting biological events that are difficult to capture with a single imaging modality. Here, to overcome this limitation, we present smartLLSM, a microscope that incorporates artificial intelligence-based instrument control to autonomously switch between epifluorescent inverted imaging and lattice light-sheet microscopy (LLSM). We apply this approach to two unique processes: cell division and immune synapse formation. In each context, smartLLSM provides population-level statistics across thousands of cells and autonomously captures multicolor three-dimensional datasets or four-dimensional time-lapse movies of rare events at rates that dramatically exceed human capabilities. From this, we quantify the effects of Taxol dose on spindle structure and kinetochore dynamics in dividing cells and of antigen strength on cytotoxic T lymphocyte engagement and lytic granule polarization at the immune synapse. Overall, smartLLSM efficiently detects rare events within heterogeneous cell populations and records these processes with high spatiotemporal four-dimensional imaging over statistically significant replicates.

## Smart microscopy principle

SmartLLSM is the survey-then-zoom paradigm lifted to a modality switch: a fast, low-dose widefield epifluorescent scan feeds a YOLOv5 object detector that classifies cells in every frame, and the instrument then autonomously repositions and acquires a full 3D / 4D lattice light-sheet stack on each hit. The low-dose screening modality and the high-content capture modality live on the same microscope, so the end-to-end loop — scan thousands of cells, detect the rare phenotype, switch mode, capture — runs without the user touching the instrument. The novelty over classical survey→zoom (Conrad 2011 Micropilot) is that both stages are on one frame, the classifier is a modern deep object detector rather than a hand-engineered phenotype rule, and the downstream modality is genuinely different optics (not just a higher NA objective) — so the photon-budget asymmetry between screen and capture is much larger.

The generalisable lesson for smart microscopy: when your target events are rare, the scout modality should be as cheap (per-cell dose, per-FOV time) as possible and the capture modality should be reserved for hits only. Any pair of modalities with that dose/content asymmetry benefits — widefield + confocal, brightfield + fluorescence, epi + structured-illumination — the control-loop shape is the same.

## Implementation on pymmcore-plus

On pymmcore-plus the mode switch is a state-device transition plus a generator that plans LLSM events from the detector's output. A cheap widefield survey runs first (MDASequence over a tile grid); a detector callback extracts per-cell world coordinates; a second generator yields per-hit LLSM acquisition events with a `CustomAction` that flips the imaging-mode state device before each capture. `core.mda.run()` handles both sweeps; `run_events` is the dispatcher.

```python
from useq import MDAEvent, MDASequence, Position, CustomAction
from src.core.hardware.core import run_events, set_objective

# Stage 1: fast, low-dose epifluorescence scan — the scout modality.
def scout_events(grid_positions, channel="epi-GFP"):
    for i, (x, y) in enumerate(grid_positions):
        yield MDAEvent(
            x_pos=x, y_pos=y,
            channel={"config": channel},
            exposure=20,                # low dose
            index={"t": 0, "p": i},
        )

hits = []  # world-coord list populated by on_frame

def on_scout_frame(img, event, meta=None):
    dets = yolo_detect(img)            # user-supplied YOLO wrapper → list of (px, py, class, score)
    sx, sy = event.x_pos, event.y_pos
    pxsz = core.getPixelSizeUm()
    H, W = img.shape
    for px, py, cls, score in dets:
        if score < 0.5 or cls != "mitotic":
            continue
        wx = sx + (px - W / 2) * pxsz
        wy = sy + (py - H / 2) * pxsz
        hits.append((wx, wy, cls, score))

# Stage 2: LLSM capture only on hits — the high-content modality.
def capture_events(hits, llsm_channel="lls-488", n_z=100, z_step=0.3):
    for i, (wx, wy, cls, score) in enumerate(hits):
        # Switch the instrument from epi to LLSM once per hit.
        yield CustomAction(
            name="set_mode",
            data={"imaging_mode": "LLSM"},  # handled by a custom action on the engine
        )
        for zi in range(n_z):
            yield MDAEvent(
                x_pos=wx, y_pos=wy,
                z_pos=(zi - n_z // 2) * z_step,
                channel={"config": llsm_channel},
                exposure=100,           # high-content, high-dose stack
                index={"p": i, "z": zi},
                metadata={"class": cls, "score": score},
            )

# Run the two stages end to end.
run_events(core, scout_events(grid_positions), on_frame=on_scout_frame)
run_events(core, capture_events(hits))
```

Production hooks in `src/core/`:

- `../../../src/core/hardware/core.py` — `run_events` is the dispatcher for both stages.
- `../../../src/core/workflows/scanning.py` — `grid_positions` + `scan_and_detect_mda` generate the scout sweep.
- `../../../src/core/workflows/adaptive.py` — `survey_cells` / `rank_by_feature` / `pixel_to_world` give the scout → world-coord → capture plumbing.
- `../../../src/core/workflows/engine.py` — `MicroscopyEngine` is where the `set_mode` custom action lives; on a real LLSM rig this drives the state device that galvos/beams the sample into light-sheet configuration. A dedicated `modality_switch.py` wrapper does not yet exist and is a clean candidate for future work.
- The detector is user-supplied: a YOLOv5 weights file for SmartLLSM, but any `image -> list[(px, py, class, score)]` function drops in — Cellpose + a classifier, a CNN, even an LLM-vision callback.

Calibration on a real prep: set the YOLO confidence threshold from a labelled scout-modality pilot (record detector score on known positives and negatives, pick the threshold that maximises recall at fixed per-scan FDR). Per-hit LLSM dose and stack depth are the smart-microscopy knobs to tune next — widefield screen dose is cheap, LLSM stacks are not.
